On Space

Matter does not exist. What exists is information about matter, written into space.
Note3:07
Addendum0:23

There used to be a recording like this once. I wonder if it survived somewhere. I think it was more detailed, more compact and clearer, because now I am trying to restore it from memory. From the memory of understanding. And something is missing.

Suppose we look at everything around — this emptiness, and me — as a kind of matrix. Not the matrix from the films, of course, but a mathematical array where everything is very simple. There are three planes. A big cube. And inside this cube, some coordinates. And I have the feeling that the direction is right. It looks like the truth.

The starting point was… I remember it exactly. There were four walls, a floor and a ceiling. Eight corners, twelve edges, six planes. And this number and difference of elements, this interpretive variety of possible states. Even the whole Universe seemed — granted, under the primacy of determinism and Laplace’s demon — a kind of enumeration of the states of this matrix.

For some reason there is also, as an axiom, the idea that nothing is identical. It fits this matrix perfectly. Because even if there were two absolutely identical bodies in two absolutely identical matrices, those matrices would have to be placed somewhere. And one matrix can no longer be the other, existing in parallel and at the same time.

So what occupies me now is the thought that the position of my body is described by a state — a kind of excitation — and by information written into these cells by exciting them. So here I am. In space there are points excited in a certain way, and this produces a state of space called “Masha”. If I start moving and change the position of my body, the neighbouring points get excited, and Masha appears somewhere nearby. And where Masha no longer is, space changes its state. The question is: will it remember that Masha was there?

Then we get a movement from state to state. But then the theory that it is all an enumeration is probably impossible. Otherwise, in each particular state, while I am in it, everything around would have to change. I changed myself or my position a little — and everything around started changing. Still, it is an amusing version.

One more critique of this version — a question not about time but about motion. If all the star systems and other macrocosms move inside this system, then at every moment I am constantly shifting along the coordinates of this very matrix. Assuming, say, that the matrix itself is still.

All right. What was the question? What is inside? If we talk about these states that make up the cube — what is inside the cube?

And at some point I realised that there is no air. There are substances that are simply the state of a region of space. Its particular excitation — as a physical term. Full stop.

Addendum

The most important thing in this update. If we perceive and consider everything as an excitation of a part of space that forms bodies in this very space in a certain way, then there is no matter as such. Matter does not exist. What exists is information about matter, written into space — about the excitation of a certain state of space. Full stop.

The Idea of Freedom Is a False Idea

Absolute freedom must imply absolute knowledge.
Note1:23
Addendum0:29

The idea of freedom is a false idea. I am not free. Not free a priori. I am not free in anything.

I am not free in expressing my thoughts, in formulating them, because I use language, words. I do not express thoughts as electrical impulses — the way I feel them. Not to mention that I do not really know how I feel them: where and with what they form, how they are held, and how they turn into verbal information.

I am not free from other people’s opinions. It does not matter whether they exist or not — they exist in my head. I am not free from myself. From my inner critic. From memories of myself, of my actions. From changes in how I see myself, the situation, my behaviour. And that is a process that is hard to predict: layers of people, thoughts, ideas, impressions, situations and experience produce myriads of thin layers and shades that can affect how any circumstance is perceived. Moreover, this picture keeps changing, shifting its shades, sometimes to the opposite.

I am not free in the sense that I am not free not to live. I am not free not to breathe. I am not free not to stick to the surface. I am not free not to sleep, not to eat, not to drink.

I am very determined. Freedom is an illusion.

Addendum

The idea of our freedom — freedom with a small letter — implies a choice between something and something. But absolute freedom must imply absolute knowledge. Our small freedom is merely the lighting-up of some nodes of options from which we can choose.

Perhaps freedom belongs only to Laplace’s demon. More precisely, to the demon himself, unfolded from an idea into some kind of reality. And he must not be physically bound to that reality.

On Personality

Perhaps personality is not the initial, a priori stage but the final one — the one that sets the goal.
Note6:27

Reflections on personality. I suppose I want to find some version for everything. Lacking real and reliable ways of empirical knowledge, the acceptable approach for now is enumeration: come up with a version and focus on it as an acceptable one. That is the selection I want to make for the notion of personality.

The most obvious one is personality as the sum of responses to external stimuli over the course of a life. Let us take a few thought experiments.

DNA and the body

Suppose personality may depend on DNA. And on physiological features that depend not only on DNA as a program but also on the real conditions of the world around us — they correct the way the program works.

The soul

Personality as the human soul. The soul is an integral, indivisible pattern, from a mythological point of view a substance of a personal nature. Immortal. Not bound to matter and, perhaps, to life — that is, not locked inside life as a path from the point of birth to the point of death. Something whole. That, I think, is the important notion: something whole, existing outside life as a process from birth to death.

Here I distinguish “life” in the common human sense — from birth to death — from life in my sense, where life is everything happening around as one whole process that includes everything. Simply everything.

In many religions and mythologies the soul, the spirit, is an important part of the concept. It is said to be a priori… though no, not a priori. It can be harmed. It can be black — in the sense of bad. Then it turns out that even outside the process of birth, life and death the soul can transform, acquiring or changing some qualities. On the whole, the concept of wholeness — personality, soul, a personal absolute — seems interesting. But the influence of a process that, on top of everything, is not controllable, on an absolute substance does not seem logical. So we will say goodbye to it.

What lies underneath

If personality is changeability, then some logic underlies the changeability. Even if the logic is not absolute but random, somewhere at the base there must be something absolute. What absolute could belong to it?

Let us limit ourselves to the human species. As a thought experiment: race, sex, lifespan and so on do not matter. What remains? Physical living conditions are extremely different — from initial data to nutrients. If we put different initial personalities into identical conditions, we get different kinds of development. Or, conversely, if we put the same personality into different initial data, we also get something.

If these are characteristics or algorithms — or both, and something else unaccounted for — then their number is most likely finite. How many can there be? Not that many, to be honest. That is why the concept of the soul seems doubtful. There are seven billion people now. Seven billion souls, then? And all of them different — fundamentally different? And that is only on Earth? And where do they go afterwards? No, I do not like it.

And yet I like the concept of the Absolute. And the concept of personality — initial entities with unique features — is very appealing. But placed into the existing reality, there are somehow too many uniquenesses. So it turns out the concept of personality has to be rejected.

Personality as the finale

Perhaps personality is not the initial, a priori stage but the final, concluding, goal-setting one? Then, by the way, it works out quite well. There are as many beings as you like. And there is potential — personalities do not exist yet, as it were. And all these beings have the possibility, the ability, the probability, the variability, the need to become these personalities.

Accepting this chain of reasoning, the path to personality lies in attaining certain qualities. And there are probably no ready-made masks waiting: “match me and you will be me”. Rather, certain qualities crystallise into absolute values.

Let us split the options. One where the frames already exist. And one where there are no frames, but there is a whole. An individual occupies a part of this whole. The next individual can also enter it — like an array of all the data from which the data of the already existing individual are excluded. Each takes some volume with them: one forty-eighth, one sixth, one half. Can someone fill all of it? Purely in theory, probably yes — by developing every trait of personality to the absolute. It is unclear how that is possible, but why not.

And if they are not predetermined but calculated by subtraction from the array, then with each new being the selection gets harder. And that is not quite fair: someone reaches it — and someone is already there. The same, or not the same, but also with absolute, only different, contradictory qualities. Something is off.

Then a simple version. There is a set, already divided, the qualities defined. Let us take one fundamental quality. Say there are eight. And this enumeration, enumeration — and someone has crossed over, and they are in the set. Then someone else. And the question is: what happens in this set when someone crosses over? In essence, I am talking about a process of deification.

Let us take historical figures who brought themselves to the absolute: Christ, the Buddha. Take the Buddha: he develops himself, teaches, books are written — and he disappears from the world. What is the point of it all, then? Probably he is simply written into a collection. Sitting somewhere, watching where everyone else is. Unclear. And why the whole enumeration then? Just a game? Or a search for these personalities? And what is it all for? A search for wholeness?

Oh, I got a little tangled. Interesting.
